# Customer line again, 16 Oct 2026 (after the nameservers moved to Gcore)

First hit of the real names through the router (`192.168.100.1`): **SERVFAIL for nureasmir.com, www, admin and probe**, repeated in about 15 ms (the failure is cached). It cleared about 4.5 minutes later.
The provider's resolver (202.163.69.21) could not resolve `ns1.gcorelabs.net` for about 9 minutes, while `ns2.gcdn.services`, Vercel's and Cloudflare's nameserver names resolved at once. All four Gcore addresses and
the Gcore zone answer correctly when asked directly from this line (30-70 ms).

The finding that explains it: **the first packet to a new address is often delayed 1-3 s on this line, for any destination and any protocol.**

* First DNS question to 13 DNS root servers: 0 slow; to the 13 `.com` registry servers: 2 slow (1.3 s and 3.2 s).
* Then TCP connect to the same 26 addresses: 4 of 26 took 1.0 s, 1.2 s, 3.1 s, 3.2 s (1 s and 3 s are TCP retransmit steps = the first SYN was lost). DNS to the same 26 now: 4 of 26 slow, one 6.8 s.
* Idle test to the nameserver addresses: Gcore 92.223.100.53 1615 ms once, Gcore 92.223.77.253 1121 ms once; Vercel ns1 and ns2 0 of 5 this time. Earlier Vercel ns1 stalled 2 of 5. No provider is immune.
* Both test lines are on the same provider (AS9541, Cyber Internet Services). No DNS interception was found (Google, Cloudflare and Quad9 answer from their own addresses).

Reading: a cold lookup needs several first contacts in a row (the resolver talking to the registry, to the nameserver names, to the nameserver), so with about 15% of first packets delayed a cold lookup often hits at least one stall; the provider's resolver then gives up,
answers SERVFAIL and remembers that for minutes. This happens with any DNS provider. Changing DNS provider changes the speed (Gcore 37 ms vs Vercel 163 ms median cold) but not this loss. What helps: fewer new addresses on the first visit (done: no font host on the
home page), longer DNS lifetime so cold lookups are rarer, and raising it with the provider/the router vendor.
